01 · Context

Context decides whether the work starts correctly.

AWhere does the source information live?
BWhich rules are written—and which live only in someone’s head?
CWhat information is allowed to be missing?
Bad context creates confident wrong work.08 / 18
02 · Work

Work needs a clear boundary.

A useful system knows what it may decide, what requires a human, and what must stop when the evidence is weak.

AWhat is the smallest complete unit of work?
BWhich decisions may the system make?
CWhere must a person remain in control?
Bounded autonomy beats vague autonomy.09 / 18
03 · Checks

Checks turn completion into something usable.

The system should test the result before asking a person to trust it.

AWhat makes the output acceptable?
BWhich failure should stop the handoff?
CWhat evidence should travel with the result?
“Done” and “safe to use” are different states.10 / 18
04 · Handoffs

A handoff is part of the system.

The next person needs the result, the reason behind it, the remaining uncertainty, and the next available action.

AWho owns the next decision?
BWhat context must survive the transfer?
CHow will the receiver know what remains Open?
The output is not finished until ownership transfers.11 / 18
05 · Recovery

Recovery keeps failure from returning to you.

A failure should arrive with what happened, what remains safe, and the next action—not as another mystery for the founder to unpack.

ACan the system retry safely?
BWhen should it ask for human judgment?
CWhat should the failure teach the next run?
Failure becomes design input.12 / 18

Fit check

This works best when the process is real.

Good fit

  • Recurring operational work still depends on the founder.
  • The team can expose the actual workflow, including failure.
  • Human ownership and judgment can be named clearly.

Not a good fit

  • The goal is AI novelty rather than an operating consequence.
  • The workflow cannot be observed or discussed honestly.
  • A guaranteed outcome is required before evidence exists.
